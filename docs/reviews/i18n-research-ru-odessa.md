# i18n research: `ru-odessa` (Odessa urban speech version of the Russian source)

Date: 2026-11-14. Output of this research: `i18n/ru-odessa/terms.md` (the rewriting guide, in Russian).
Nothing else was changed; `i18n/languages.json` has no `ru-odessa` entry yet.

Spelling note: the city is "Odessa" here because the language code and the Russian-language scholarship use
that form; the current official English transliteration is "Odesa" (the English Wikipedia article is
"Odesan Russian").

## What the variety is

A Russian-based urban koine, formed in the first third of the 19th century, with Ukrainian and Yiddish as the
main substrates and Polish, Greek, Italian, French, Romanian/Moldovan and Turkic lexical layers. Linguists
disagree on the label (regional variant of Russian, urban koine, Jewish ethnolect, contact variety). All the
serious sources agree on three points that shaped the guide:

1. The historically attested variety has declined since the mid-20th century; younger speakers use mostly
   standard Russian (Khmelevsky and Knyazkova 2019; Savchenko and Khmelevsky 2020; Grenoble).
2. Much of the available "evidence" is fiction, feuilleton and humour, which exaggerate (Grenoble; Kabanen 2021).
3. Today the marked variety is "mostly used for comic effect" (Verschik 2007) and the version reproduced
   outside the city is partly artificial (Kabanen 2021).

So the guide takes only features that linguists describe, uses literary and lexicographic examples to
illustrate them, and treats the humour tradition as something to ration.

## The line between attested features, stage cliché and caricature

**(a) Attested by linguists, used in the guide.** Government: "за" + accusative with speech verbs, "скучать
за", "смеяться с", causal "через", "до" for "к". "Иметь" for possession and in the fixed formula. Particles
"таки", "же ж", "аж", "так" = "то", "или" = "ли", "бо". Question-for-answer. Object-first order.
Demonstrative plus possessive. Ethical dative in formulas. "Делать" + noun. Calques that linguists trace to
Yiddish and that are now ordinary colloquial Russian ("не берите в голову", "спрашивается вопрос").

**(b) Stage and anecdote register, used sparingly.** "Две большие разницы", "слушайте сюда", "я вас
умоляю", "на минуточку", "или!", "ша", "чтоб я так жил", "будьте мне здоровы". All are attested (Babel,
Zhvanetsky, Utyosov, *Крокодил* 1911–1912, Rothstein), but they are the items everyone recognizes, so the
guide caps each at once per module.

**(c) Excluded, with the reason.**

| Excluded | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Eye-dialect for accent | "риба", "ви", "мине", "чтоби" | Kabanen classes exactly this (Averchenko 1911) as stylized stereotype; in print it reads as mocking a Yiddish accent. Verschik notes such phonetic markers index "Jewish speech" for outsiders |
| Broken grammar as a device | "ложка для кушать", "вы будете купить", "не сохните здесь бельё", "вас здесь не стояло", "невыносимый грязь" | Attested, but as learner interference and as feuilleton material. In an instruction they damage meaning and present speakers as incompetent |
| Jokes built on ethnicity | the "Иванов, несмотря на фамилию, таки да" type | Stereotype humour about a group |
| Religious and in-group Yiddish and Hebrew words | "азохен вей", "вей з мир", "лэхаим", "шабес", "кошерно", "мишпуха", "шлимазл" | From an outside narrator they are mimicry. Neutral Yiddish-origin words with a general colloquial life were kept with a gloss requirement (цимес, хохма, гешефт, бекицер, гембель, халоймес, майсы) |
| Ethnonyms and slurs | "пиндос" and the ethnonym entries of the dictionary | Offensive |
| Vulgar words | "поц", "шмок", "тухес" | Vulgar. "Поц" is explicitly banned as the task required |
| Curses and health oaths | "шоб вам пальцы отпали", "чтобы он был так здоров", "шоб вы так жили" | Ill-wishing; meaning flips with intonation, which text does not carry |
| Criminal argot | "ксива", "шухер", "шнифт", "туфта", "фуфло", "хипешить", "нычка" | Argot, not city colloquial |

Modal "иметь" does not distinguish "must" from "may" (Stepanov gives both readings) |

## Decisions and rejected variants

1. **Address: polite singular "вы"** (source uses "ты"). Reason: the carrying formulas are attested in the
   "вы" form, and polite address plus colloquial irony is the register itself. Rejected: keeping "ты"
   (attested too, e.g. "слушай сюда", but it forces re-forming the formulas and reads more familiar than the
   source); addressing the reader as "мадам / месье" (historical, gendered, and close to parody).
2. **Spelling: standard orthography; "шо" / "шоб" only inside four listed formulas.** Reason: "шо" is
   described by linguists as a real pronoun form and is a general southern colloquial form, so it is a
   lexical marker, not accent imitation; blanket replacement would turn the text into eye-dialect. Rejected:
   no "шо" at all (loses the single most recognizable marker); "шо" everywhere; the "ше / щё" form of the
   dictionary and of Khmelevsky (less readable; "шё" is given as the Odessan form by the English Wikipedia,
   so the sources do not agree on one spelling).
3. **Safety text and diagnostics get no colour at all**, only the address change. The diagnostics markers
   stay as in the source because `FIELD_KEYS` in `scripts/build_site.py` matches the Russian keys.
4. **Density: at most two marked constructions per paragraph**, each headline formula once per module.
5. **Restricted government.** "За" only with four speech verbs; causal "через" only with abstract nouns;
   "до" only as "до нас / до вас". Each restriction exists because the same preposition is literal
   elsewhere in the course ("через резистор", "за 5 секунд").
6. **Lexicon: 112 entries**, each with a source tag. Low-transparency words carry a "with gloss" condition.

## Items left out of terms.md as unconfirmed

Searched for and not found in any opened source, so not in the guide: "вы будете смеяться", "слушайте меня
ушами", "знать за" (as in "Беня знает за облаву"), "вы хочете песен — их есть у меня", "что-то
особенного", "не крутите мне голову" in Russian form (Rothstein gives only the half-Yiddish "не дрейте мне
копф"), "за это" in the sense "поэтому" (hromadske mentions it as a confusion, without analysis), genitive
"таких дел" (hromadske only).

Present in terms.md in an adapted form, which a reviewer should know:
- "за это и поговорим" and "тудой-сюдой" are built on attested models ("говорить за"; "тудою и сюдою",
  "тудой / сюдой"), not found as fixed strings.
- "шо такое?" and "шоб вы знали" combine the dictionary's phrases ("ше такое", "чтоб вы знали") with the
  "шо / шоб" form attested elsewhere.
- "шо вы мне говорите!", "расскажете это бабушке", "не морочьте голову" are "вы" forms of entries attested
  with "ты".
- "с гаком" is the standard spelling of the dictionary's "з гаком".

## Confidence

- High: government features, "иметь", "таки", question-for-answer. Several independent academic sources
  with the same examples.
- Medium: the lexicon. About two thirds of the entries rest on one citation-based popular dictionary
  ([СО]); the meanings were read through an automatic summary of the page, in three passes, and a second
  pass confirmed the key entries with quotations. A few glosses there look loose ("фортель", "чмара") and
  those entries were not used.
- Medium: Rothstein, read through a blog's long quotation, not the article.
- Low: "на полном серьёзе" (one non-academic note) and the claim in the Russian Wikipedia mirror that
  "говорить за" is a Bulgarian calque (other sources say Ukrainian; the guide follows Stepanov, Rothstein).

## Open questions

1. **Interface strings.** `ru-odessa` over `ru` inherits `ui.json` and `data.json` strings that address the
   reader as "ты". With "вы" in the modules the interface and the prose will disagree unless the address
   strings in the catalogs are also rewritten. Decide before the rewrite starts.
2. **Who reviews.** No native speaker of the variety has read the guide. A reader from Odesa should check
   the sample pairs and the lexicon for tone before modules are published.
3. **Reception.** The variety is tied to a living city and to a largely emigrated Jewish community. The
   guide avoids caricature by rule, but the page should say plainly that this is an affectionate stylization
   of literary Odessa speech, as the Galician version is labelled an experiment.
4. **Language tag.** `ru-odessa` is well-formed BCP 47 (a 6-letter variant subtag) but not registered, same
   status as `uk-galych`. Name and short label for the picker are not decided.
5. **Quiz and task banks.** The guide allows only the address change there. Whether to copy those banks at
   all, or let them fall through to the source with "ты", depends on question 1.
6. **Primary sources.** Stepanov 2004, Mechkovskaya 2006, Smirnov's dictionary and the Ukrainian Wikipedia
   article remain unread; if any becomes available, the lexicon entries resting only on [СО] are the ones to
   re-check.
