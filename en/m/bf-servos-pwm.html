Сервы, PWM и таймеры: `resource SERVO`, `servo`, `smix`, SERVO_TILT и channel forwarding

BetaflightL2 — Integratorreading 50 minstudy ~3 hBetaflight 2025.12 (сверено с 2025.12.5); лабораторная снята на исходнике 4.5.x (ветка 4.5-maintenance, 7dcc851; version.h 4.5.6, последний выпуск 4.5.5)MASTERED

A servo on a Betaflight quad is not “plug in and it works”: the firmware has to support servos (in Cloud Build — the SERVOS option), the output has to sit on a timer that is not used by DShot, the power has to come from a separate BEC, and smix is not applied on a quad at all. This module separates three different kinds of “PWM” (motor PWM/OneShot, DShot, the 1000–2000 µs servo pulse at 50–333 Hz), explains STM32/AT32 timers and channels, how to read timer, resource, dma show and how to move a servo to a free pad, goes through the servo and smix commands from the source (the same in 4.5 and 2025.12) and the SERVO_TILT and CHANNEL_FORWARDING features, and builds a simple pan/tilt gimbal. The lab: a Python script parses the real SpeedyBee F405 V4 config from the 4.5-config branch and the F405 timer table from the source, finds the pads that can do 50 Hz with DShot600 on the motors, and computes the servo pulse with the servos.c formulas; then the bench without props, a servo on a separate BEC, a tester or an oscilloscope, and a gimbal test.

  • настроить servo, SERVO_TILT и channel forwarding и посчитать импульс сервы
  • запитать серву от отдельного BEC и проверить импульс прибором

Theory

Three different kinds of “PWM” on one board

A beginner calls everything that comes out of a pad “PWM”. In fact these are three different signals:

SignalWhat is transmittedPeriod / frequencyIn Betaflight 2025.12
Motor PWMa 1000–2000 µs pulsemotor_pwm_rate 480 Hz (200–32000); for PWM always asynchronous and no higher than 480 Hz, for OneShot/Multishot — only with use_unsynced_pwmmotor_pwm_protocol = PWM, old ESCs with calibration

DShot150/300/600a digital 16-bit frame with a CRC; DShot600 = 600 kbit/s, a 1.667 µs bit, a frame of ≈ 26.7 µsonce per PID loop, there is no “frequency”DSHOT600 by default; on F4/G4 with bidir the firmware sets DSHOT300 by itself (config/config.c)
Servo pulsea width of 1000–2000 µs (center 1500) sets the angle50 Hz (20 ms) for analog servos, up to ~333 Hz for digital onesservo_pwm_rate 50 (50–498)

DShot is not PWM: the ESC reads bits, not the pulse width. A servo does not care how often the pulse arrives (within its specification); what matters is the width. An analog servo at 333 Hz may hum and heat up — set a frequency above 50 Hz only for digital servos and per the datasheet (the servo_pwm_rate description in INAV says that most digital servos support about 330 Hz). At 498 Hz the period is 2008 µs — a 2000 pulse still fits, but the CLI servo limit (up to 2500 µs) no longer does.

PWM *inputs* are alive too: feature RX_PPM and RX_PARALLEL_PWM (input_filtering_mode, OFF by default) capture the receiver pulses with a timer in input capture mode. USE_RX_PPM/USE_RX_PWM (in both 4.5 and 2025.12) are in the non-cloud block of common_pre.h, and they are not in the option list of the Cloud Build API. For a cloud build, assume they do not exist.

Timer, channel, pad

In STM32 (and AT32F435) PWM is generated by a hardware timer TIMx: a counter with a prescaler PSC and a period ARR. A timer has up to 4 channels CH1–CH4 (TIM1/TIM8 also have the complementary CH1N–CH3N), and each channel is routed to one or two pins through an alternate function AF. A channel selects only the pulse width (the CCR register), while the period and the clocking are shared by the whole timer. Hence the main rule: all the outputs of one timer run one protocol at one frequency.

The numbers from the lab (F405, a timer on the 84 MHz bus):

  • DShot600: PSC 6, ARR 19 → a 1.667 µs bit (MOTOR_DSHOT600_HZ 12 MHz, MOTOR_BITLENGTH 20);
  • a 50 Hz servo: PSC 83, ARR 19999 → a 1 µs tick, a 20 ms frame (servoDevInit() → pwmOutConfig(…, PWM_TIMER_1MHZ, 1 MHz / servo_pwm_rate, …)).

One timer cannot hold both modes. Betaflight does not check this: timerAllocate() refuses only if the same pin-channel is taken, and hands out a neighboring channel of the same timer without question. The servos are initialized after the motors (fc/init.c: motorDevInit before servoDevInit), so a servo on a channel of a motor timer will overwrite its time base, and DShot on those motors will break. The same goes for everything that lives on a timer: LED_STRIP (800 kHz + DMA), CAMERA_CONTROL (hardware PWM at 84 MHz / 128), PPM.

DShot comes in two kinds. Timer + DMA: every motor pad is a channel of its timer plus a DMA stream. Bitbang: the motors are toggled as GPIO through DMA, and the clock is set by a “pacer” timer, TIM1 or TIM8 (dshot_bitbang_timer AUTO/TIM1/TIM8). With bitbang the timers of the motor pads are free, but TIM1/TIM8 must not be touched. dshot_bitbang = AUTO on F4 enables bitbang only together with dshot_bidir, and on F7/H7/G4/AT32 — always (isDshotBitbangActive()). The board config can override this: the SpeedyBee F405 V4 has DEFAULT_DSHOT_BITBANG = OFF. A servo does not need DMA; the dmaopt of a servo pad may be −1.

How to read the board map

  • resource — which functions are assigned to which pins (resource SERVO 1 B15). resource show — who actually owns the pins after boot, resource show all — plus timer show and dma show.
  • timer show — which channels of which timers are taken and by what (TIM12: CH2 : SERVO 1), timer <pin> list — which timers a pin has (# AF9: TIM12 CH2), timer <pin> AF<n> — select one. timer list without a pin prints “NOT IMPLEMENTED YET” (in both 4.5 and 2025.12).
  • dma show — the DMA streams in use, dma pin <pin> <opt> — change the stream.
  • Numbering: resource SERVO and resource MOTOR count from 1, while servo, smix and the Servos tab count from 0. resource SERVO 1 = servo 0 in the tab.
  • resource SERVO 2 B10, when B10 holds MOTOR 7, only prints NOTE: B10 already assigned to MOTOR 7. and does not unassign the motor. Free it explicitly: resource MOTOR 7 NONE.
  • servoDevInit() takes SERVO 1, 2, 3… in order and stops at the first empty one. SERVO 1 NONE + SERVO 2 B10 = no servos at all.

Power: never from the flight controller's 5 V (except a micro servo)

A servo is a motor with a gearbox. An MG996R draws 500–900 mA when moving and 2.5 A at stall at 6 V per its datasheet. Two such servos at stall — 5 A. The flight controller's BEC is rated for a few amps (see the datasheet of your board) and already feeds the receiver, camera and GPS. A sag of the 5 V rail reboots the MCU in the air. The rules:

  1. Power a servo larger than a micro (the 9 g class) from a separate BEC of 5–6 V, or 7.4–8.4 V for HV servos, with a current rating ≥ the sum of the stall currents.
  2. A common ground of the BEC, the servo and the flight controller is mandatory: the signal is measured relative to the flight controller's ground.
  3. Only the signal (and ground) goes from the flight controller to the servo. The positive supply comes from the BEC.
  4. An electrolytic capacitor of 470–1000 µF on the servo rail at the connector damps the surges at start. If a servo twitches with throttle — look for the common ground and interference, not a “firmware bug”.
  5. Servos move while disarmed too: writeServos() runs every PID loop without checking the arm state (except the tricopter tail servo with tri_unarmed_servo = OFF). A gimbal will jerk when power is applied.

Servos in Betaflight 2025.12: what is built in and how the outputs are handed out

USE_SERVOS is in the #if !defined(CLOUD_BUILD) && !defined(SITL) block of target/common_pre.h. A local “full” build has servos, while Cloud Build has them only with the SERVOS option (Other Options in the Cloud Build API) or with the WING package, which enables USE_SERVOS by itself (WING — from 2025.12; in 4.5 only SERVOS). SITL has no servos at all (neither in 4.5 nor in 2025.12), so the lab is a model in Python. No servo/smix commands in the CLI and no Servos tab means the option is not in the build. Up to 8 servos (MAX_SUPPORTED_SERVOS), 1 with USE_QUAD_MIXER_ONLY.

Servos are enabled if the mixer requires them (TRI, FLYING_WING, AIRPLANE, CUSTOMAIRPLANE, CUSTOMTRI, GIMBAL…) or if feature SERVO_TILT / feature CHANNEL_FORWARDING is on. The order of the physical outputs is set by writeServos() (the model output, step 3):

  • first the mixer servos (for CUSTOMAIRPLANE: SERVO 1 ← servo[2] FLAPS, SERVO 2 ← servo[3] FLAPPERON_1, … SERVO 6 ← servo[7] THROTTLE);
  • then the two gimbal servos (servo[0] — pitch, servo[1] — roll), with SERVO_TILT;
  • then the AUX channels, with CHANNEL_FORWARDING.

`smix` does nothing on a quad. servoTable() calls servoMixer() only for airplane and other “servo” mixers. Custom smix rules are loaded only for CUSTOMAIRPLANE and CUSTOMTRI (servoConfigureOutput()). On QUADX the gimbal works by the separate SERVO_TILT formula.

servo: endpoints, rate, forward

servo <i> <min> <max> <middle> <rate> <forward> — 6 numbers, i = 0…7. The default is servo 0 1000 2000 1500 100 -1. The CLI requires 500 ≤ min ≤ middle ≤ max ≤ 2500 and a rate of −100…100. forward is the index of an RC channel from 0 (4 = AUX1, 5 = AUX2), −1 — none. The betaflight.com documentation (Mixer) gives the old syntax with angleMin/angleMax — it exists in neither 4.5 nor 2025.12. If forward is set, the channel, converted from 1000…2000 to min…max, is taken instead of middle (determineServoMiddleOrForwardFromChannel()). The result is always clipped to min…max. The SERVO_TILT gimbal is reversed with a negative rate. A forward channel is reversed on the radio: the CLI will not accept min > max.

servo_center_pulse (1500) is used only in servoDevInit() as the initial channel value, and pwmOutConfig() zeroes the CCR right away. The center is set by middle in servo.

smix: the servo mixer rules

smix <rule> <servo> <source> <rate> <speed> <min> <max> <box>: rule 0…15 (MAX_SERVO_RULES = 2 × 8), servo 0…7, source 0…13, rate −100…100, speed 0…255, min/max 0…100 (min < max), box 0…3. The sources: 0–2 — stabilized roll/pitch/yaw (pidSum × 0.7, in PASSTHRU — the stick command), 3 — throttle, 4–7 — RC roll/pitch/yaw/throttle, 8–11 — AUX1–AUX4, 12–13 — gimbal pitch/roll. The rules are summed. Every rule is clipped to min…max percent of the travel of the target servo (0 % = min, 50 % = center). The sum is multiplied by the rate from servo and added to middle. Details of the code:

  • a rule with rate = 0 terminates the table: everything after it is ignored;
  • speed is a step per PID loop: speed 10 at 8 kHz goes 0 → 500 in 6.2 ms, practically without slowing;
  • smix reverse <servo> <source> r|n flips the sign after the rule limit; smix reset erases the rules and all the reverses;
  • box 1–3 — the rule works only in the SERVO1–3 mode (present in Modes only with CUSTOMAIRPLANE).

The table of servo numbers on the Mixer page (2 = ELEVATOR, 6 = THROTTLE, 7 = FLAPS) does not match servoIndex_e (4.5 and 2025.12): 2 = FLAPS, 3/4 = FLAPPERON_1/2, 5 = RUDDER, 6 = ELEVATOR, 7 = THROTTLE. Trust the source.

SERVO_TILT, CAMSTAB and channel forwarding

feature SERVO_TILT gives two gimbal servos and the CAMSTAB mode (id 8) in Modes. Without CAMSTAB the servos stay at middle (or follow the forward channel). With CAMSTAB in gimbal_mode = NORMAL (the default):

pulse = constrain(middle_or_forward + rate × angle_in_tenths_of_a_degree / 50, min, max)

That is, rate/5 µs per degree of frame tilt. For a servo that travels 90° per 1000 µs (11.1 µs/°), a rate of 5 × 11.1 ≈ 56 is needed. With rate 100 the gimbal overcompensates by a factor of 1.8 and hits 1000/2000 already at ±25°. MIXTILT mixes pitch and roll for a differential gimbal. If the camera goes the wrong way — change the sign of rate.

feature CHANNEL_FORWARDING outputs the AUX channels on the remaining servo outputs as is, starting from channel_forwarding_start (4 = AUX1 by default). The values are not limited by min/max — the endpoints are set on the radio. With SERVO_TILT forwarding starts from SERVO 3.

Place in the system

  • Receives: a free timer not used by DShot and a pad from the board map (bf-cli); the SERVOS option in the build (bf-custom); power from a separate BEC with a common ground (el-power-design); the CAMSTAB mode from a switch (bf-modes-osd).
  • Provides: the 1000–2000 µs servo pulse to the gimbal; the servo[0..7] record in the log (bf-blackbox); the rule “one timer — one protocol”, shared with the LED strip and the motors (bf-led-buzzer).
  • If you get it wrong here: a servo is assigned to a channel of a motor timer → it overwrites the time base, and DShot on those motors breaks; the firmware does not check this, and it looks like an ESC failure (f-motors-esc, bf-cli). A servo is powered from the flight controller's 5 V → at stall the rail sags, and the MCU reboots in the air (el-power-design).

Practice

The main path is a Python model. The script and the output were captured on the 4.5.x source (the 4.5-maintenance branch, commit 7dcc851: version.h says 4.5.6, but there is no such release; the latest release is 4.5.5, and the src/ code matches it except for the version number in version.h) and the config of the 4.5-config branch; the servos.c formulas (servoTable(), servoMixer(), writeServos()) did not change in 2025.12, while the timer drivers moved from src/main/drivers/stm32/ to src/platform/STM32/, so the path to timer_stm32f4xx.c is different for the 2025.12 tree. Betaflight SITL (4.5 and 2025.12) is built without USE_SERVOS (the !defined(SITL) condition in common_pre.h), and building SITL in this environment is prohibited by policy anyway. The script bf-servos-pwm.servo_lab.py (the course working folder, stage 6) reads the board config from betaflight/config of the 4.5-config branch and the 4.5 source: the fullTimerHardware[] table (timer_stm32f4xx.c) and the constants of servos.h, servos.c, dshot_dpwm.h. It then repeats timerGetByTagAndIndex(), servoDevInit(), writeServos(), servoTable() and servoMixer() with C integer arithmetic. Everything below is an actual run.

Step 1. The timer map of a real board

python3 -I bf-servos-pwm.servo_lab.py configs/SPEEDYBEEF405V4/config.h ~/src/betaflight-4.5/src/main

The core of the parsing is the occurrence number of the pin in the timer table (TIMER_PIN_MAP(i, pin, occurrence, dmaopt)):

def timer_by_occurrence(rows, pin, occ):
    n = 0
    for p, tim, ch in rows:
        if p == pin:
            n += 1
            if n == occ:
                return tim, ch

The conclusion: for a gimbal with two servos the best pads are S9 (TIM12, after the camera is unassigned) and M7 (TIM2). M7 is safer than M5: TIM2 touches neither DShot on TIM3/TIM4 nor bitbang, should someone enable it later. A ready config (the answer to the frequent question “how do I move a servo to a free pad without breaking DShot”):

resource CAMERA_CONTROL NONE
resource MOTOR 7 NONE
resource SERVO 2 B10
feature SERVO_TILT
servo 0 1000 2000 1500 56 -1
servo 1 1000 2000 1500 56 -1
save

After the reboot timer show should show TIM2: CH3 : SERVO 2, TIM12: CH2 : SERVO 1, and the motors on TIM3/TIM4 (the line format is per showTimers() in cli.c).

Step 3. What ends up on each output

QUADX + SERVO_TILT + CHANNEL_FORWARDING:
   SERVO 1 <- servo[0] GIMBAL_PITCH; SERVO 2 <- servo[1] GIMBAL_ROLL; SERVO 3 <- rcData[4] = AUX1 raw, not constrained; SERVO 4 <- rcData[5] = AUX2 raw, not constrained ...
CUSTOMAIRPLANE (no features):
   SERVO 1 <- servo[2] FLAPS; SERVO 2 <- servo[3] FLAPPERON_1; SERVO 3 <- servo[4] FLAPPERON_2; SERVO 4 <- servo[5] RUDDER ...

SERVO_TILT has no camera pan (yaw). For pan/tilt without stabilization CHANNEL_FORWARDING is simpler: the AUX1 pot → SERVO 1 (tilt), AUX2 → SERVO 2 (pan). For tilt with stabilization — SERVO_TILT, and pan through forwarding lands on SERVO 3.

Step 4. The gimbal pulse in CAMSTAB

servo: 90 deg over 1000 us -> 11.11 us/deg; firmware adds rate/5 us per deg -> rate = 5*11.11 = 56

With rate 100 at a 20° tilt the servo goes to 36° — the camera “dips” the other way. Rate 56 holds the horizon with an error of ≤ 0.3°. A forward channel (servo 0 1000 2000 1500 56 4) lets the pilot set the tilt angle with a pot, and stabilization is added on top.

Step 5. smix on an airplane mixer

The elevons of a flying wing from RC sources: smix 0 3 4 100 0 0 100 0, smix 1 3 5 100 0 0 100 0, smix 2 4 4 -100 0 0 100 0, smix 3 4 5 100 0 0 100 0.

RC roll 1700 pitch 1300 -> servo3 (SERVO 2 pad) 1500 us, servo4 (SERVO 3 pad) 1100 us
servo 3 1100 1900 1480 60 -1, roll 1700 pitch 1300 -> servo3 1480 us (rate 60 % of 0, middle 1480)
same servo, roll 2000 pitch 2000 -> servo3 1900 us (each rule clamped to +-400 = width/2, 800*60/100 = 480, 1480+480 = 1960 -> max 1900)
rule min 30 max 70 (width 1000 -> limits -200..+200), roll 2000 -> servo3 1700 us
smix reverse 4 5 r, pitch 1300 -> servo3 1300 us, servo4 1700 us (servo 4 pitch reversed)
rule 1 with rate 0 -> table ends there: 1 of 4 rules active
speed 10, PID 8000 Hz: 0 -> 500 (1500 -> 2000 us) in 50 loops =    6.2 ms

The chain is: the rule → clipping to the rule's min/max → the sum → × the servo rate → + middle → clipping to the servo's min/max. Servo 3 (FLAPPERON_1) comes out on the SERVO 2 pad: the first physical output is taken by FLAPS.

Moving to real hardware

The bench, props off, the battery through a current limiter.

  1. diff all → a “before” file. Check the build: servo and smix are in help (the option list — by the BUILD KEY from status). If not — rebuild with SERVOS.
  2. Enter the config of step 2 for your own board: first timer <pin> list and resource show all. The servo pad must not share a timer with the motors, LED_STRIP, CAMERA_CONTROL or the bitbang pacer. After save check timer show.
  3. Power: a separate 5–6 V BEC (per the servo), the BEC ground ↔ the flight controller's GND, the signal from the pad. A 470–1000 µF capacitor on the rail. With a multimeter, at stall (hold the servo horn by hand for 1 s), write down the current. It must be below the BEC rating.
  4. The pulse: a servo tester in measurement mode or an oscilloscope/logic analyzer on the pad. Expected: a period of 20.0 ms (50 Hz), a width of 1500 ± 5 µs at the center. The radio → AUX: 1000/2000 µs at the ends of the forward channel.
  5. Betaflight App → Servos (numbered from 0) or Motors: the values match the measurement. Choose min/max so that the servo does not hit the mechanical stop: hum and current at the end are a sign of a stop. Shorten the travel by 20–50 µs.
  6. The motors: in the Motors tab without props every motor spins from its own slider, and there are no DShot errors. The servo does not twitch while this happens.
  7. The gimbal: feature SERVO_TILT, CAMSTAB on a switch. Tilt the frame by hand by 20° in pitch — the camera should stay level (a spirit level or a level app on the camera, a tolerance of ±3°). If it goes twice as far — reduce rate. If it goes the wrong way — change the sign.

Abort: a smell, a hot BEC or servo, a flight controller reboot when the servo moves, the motors twitch or do not arm after resource, the servo hums at rest for longer than 2 s — remove power and restore the “before” diff all.

Flight (optional): a hover at 1–2 m in ANGLE, CAMSTAB on. Abort: the picture shakes more than without stabilization (enable servo_lowpass_hz 20–40), a flight controller reset, a 5 V sag in the OSD.

Self-check: the lab is complete when:

  • (model) the script has been run on the config of your own or the teaching board and the source (write down the version); the timer table is saved in your notes (for SPEEDYBEEF405V4 it matches step 1), and a servo pad is chosen for which the “shares timer with” column contains no motor, LED_STRIP, CAMERA_CONTROL or bitbang pacer;
  • (model) the SERVO_TILT rate for your own servo is computed (rate = 5 × µs/° from the datasheet) along with the pulse at tilts of 10° and 20°; the check of the formula — an 11.11 µs/° servo gives a rate of 56, 1612 and 1724 µs;
  • (hardware) timer show after save shows the servo on its own timer, the motors have no errors, and the oscilloscope or tester shows 20 ms and 1500 ± 5 µs;
  • (hardware) the servo is powered from a separate BEC with a common ground, the stall current is written down, and a 20° frame tilt is compensated with an error of ≤ 3°.

Reference for self-check. A pad does not break DShot if its timer serves no motor: one timer cannot hold both the DShot600 time base (PSC 6, ARR 19) and the 50 Hz frame (PSC 83, ARR 19999). For the teaching board the correct answer is S9 on TIM12 after resource CAMERA_CONTROL NONE and M7 on TIM2, and timer show shows TIM2: CH3 : SERVO 2 and TIM12: CH2 : SERVO 1. If M5 was chosen — with dshot_bitbang enabled TIM8 is taken by the pacer. If after the move two motors do not spin or twitch — the servo pad is on a channel of a motor timer. If the pulse at 20° came out as 1900 µs and the camera went to 36° — rate was left at 100 instead of the computed value.

Troubleshooting

Symptom: the CLI has no servo/smix commands, and there is no Servos tab

  • Possible cause: a Cloud Build without the SERVOS option (USE_SERVOS is only in the non-cloud block of common_pre.h).
  • What to do: rebuild with SERVOS, restore diff all.

Symptom: the servo does not move, and resource looks correct

  • Possible cause: SERVO 1 is empty (servoDevInit stops at the first empty one), the feature is not enabled or the mixer has no servos, the pin is still held by MOTOR n.
  • What to do: resource show — the owner of the pin must be SERVO; assign from SERVO 1 in order; resource MOTOR n NONE; feature SERVO_TILT or CHANNEL_FORWARDING.

Symptom: after a servo was moved two motors do not spin or twitch

  • Possible cause: the servo pad is on a channel of a motor timer — servoDevInit() overwrote the DShot time base.
  • What to do: timer show, move the servo to a timer without motors (on the SpeedyBee F405 V4 — TIM2/M7).

Symptom: smix was entered, but nothing happens on a quad

  • Possible cause: smix rules are applied only in CUSTOMAIRPLANE/CUSTOMTRI; a rule with rate 0 terminates the table.
  • What to do: for a gimbal — SERVO_TILT and servo … rate; for an airplane — mixer CUSTOMAIRPLANE, the rules in order from 0.

Symptom: the camera is stabilized too strongly or in the opposite direction

  • Possible cause: rate 100 gives 20 µs/°; the sign is wrong.
  • What to do: rate = 5 × (the servo's µs/°), the sign — from a tilt test.

Symptom: the servo hits the stop and hums at the ends of the forward channel

  • Possible cause: CHANNEL_FORWARDING outputs RC as is, and the servo min/max do not apply.
  • What to do: limit the travel on the radio (the channel endpoints) or switch to SERVO_TILT with the forward field.

Symptom: the flight controller reboots when the servo moves

  • Possible cause: the servo is on the flight controller's 5 V, a sag at the stall current.
  • What to do: a separate BEC, a common ground, a capacitor; check the stall current.
  • Consequences: in flight — a disarm and a crash.

Symptom: the camera buttons through CAMERA_CONTROL stopped working after the servo was set up

  • Possible cause: CAMERA_CONTROL in HARDWARE_PWM on the same timer (on the SpeedyBee F405 V4 — TIM12).
  • What to do: resource CAMERA_CONTROL NONE or set camera_control_mode = SOFTWARE_PWM (available on F405), or the servo on another timer.

Symptom: an analog servo heats up and hums

  • Possible cause: servo_pwm_rate is above its specification.
  • What to do: return to 50 Hz; 200–333 Hz — only for digital servos per the datasheet.

Notes

The Mixer page on betaflight.com is out of date in its servo part relative to the 4.5 and 2025.12 code: the servo syntax with angleMin/angleMax (in the code — 6 numbers), servo_lowpass_freq/servo_lowpass_enable (in the code — a single servo_lowpass_hz), the table of servo numbers for smix (in the code 2 = FLAPS, 6 = ELEVATOR, 7 = THROTTLE), “speed per loop (1 ms default)” (the PID loop is now 8 kHz), “smix reverse per-profile” (the reverses are in servoParams, and there are no servo profiles). The Servos & Servo Tilt page is right about resource numbering from 1, but its examples for F1/F3 (NAZE32, SPRacing F3) do not apply to 4.5. The course audit assumed a servo lab in SITL. SITL (4.5 and 2025.12) is built without USE_SERVOS, so a model is used here.
